# Bass Sounds: Theory

*Exploration 01* — how the Deep808Lab bass synthesizer works, and why.

All figures are generated by `docs/generate_figures.py` (`uv run python docs/generate_figures.py`).

---

## What we're trying to build

Most "808 generators" are just a sine wave through a distortion. That misses
most of what makes a modern sub bass feel *expensive*. The sound is not one
thing — it is several interacting components:

```
Sine Oscillator
        |
        v
Pitch Envelope      (punch: the pitch drops exponentially)
        |
        v
Amplitude Envelope  (ADSR: fast attack, long decay)
        |
        v
Warmth Layers       (octave sine, triangle, low-order harmonics)
        |
        v
Oversample (4x)     (push aliasing out of the audible band)
        |
        v
Analog Saturation   (tanh drive -> harmonics for small speakers)
        |
        v
Tone Low-pass       (one-pole, keeps it from getting buzzy)
        |
        v
Soft Clipper        (catches peaks, barely colors)
        |
        v
Look-ahead Limiter  (prevents clipping)
        |
        v
Normalize -> 24-bit WAV (48 kHz, mono)
```

Each stage is individually understandable. Five primary knobs drive the
whole thing; everything else is a sensible default.

## The five primary controls

### 1. Frequency — logarithmic, not linear

Pitch perception is logarithmic: an octave up doubles the frequency. A
linear knob between 25 and 70 Hz would bury most of its range in the same
few usable notes. So the knob position maps logarithmically:

```
f(position) = exp(log(25) + position * (log(70) - log(25)))
```

![Frequency knob mapping](figures/freq_log.png)

### 2. Punch — the pitch envelope

Punch is what separates an 808 from a plain sine. At the very start of the
note the pitch is briefly *higher* than the fundamental, then slides down
exponentially. The pitch envelope is a multiplier on the fundamental:

```
f(t) = f0 * (1 + (start_ratio - 1) * exp(-t / decay))
```

Higher punch = higher starting pitch and a slower, more audible slide.

![Pitch envelopes for various punch settings](figures/pitch_envelope.png)

### 3. Drive — oversampled analog saturation

Saturation is where harmonics come from. A pure 40 Hz sine is inaudible on
laptop speakers; drive generates harmonics so the note is felt and heard.
We use `tanh`, never hard clipping:

```
y = tanh(gain * x),   gain = 1 + 8 * drive
```

Because `tanh` is smooth, its harmonics roll off gracefully. To avoid
digital aliasing, the whole chain runs at 4x the sample rate: we oversample,
saturate, then low-pass and decimate back.

![Saturation curves](figures/saturation.png)

### 4. Warmth — harmonic complexity

Warmth fades in a set of extra components *on top of* the fundamental:
an octave-up sine, a subtle triangle, and low-order (3rd/4th) harmonics.
Each component gates in from silence as warmth goes 0 -> 1, so at 0 the
voice is a pure sine and at 1 it is a rich "velvet" tone. The levels are
deliberately low and tuned to never sound buzzy.

![Warmth component amplitudes](figures/warmth_harmonics.png)

### 5. Weight — the macro knob

Weight is the missing "magic" knob. It does not control one DSP block; it
moves several at once to change how *physically large* the bass feels:

| What it adjusts         | Lean (0)              | Heavy (1)             |
|-------------------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|
| Envelope decay          | short, tight          | long, lingering       |
| Sub oscillator level    | ~ -4 dB               | unity                 |
| Tone low-pass cutoff    | higher / brighter     | lower / darker        |
| Limiter threshold       | high                  | lower (more glue)     |

![Weight macro behavior](figures/weight_macro.png)

Lean -> Focused -> Huge -> Earthquake.

## Signal flow details

### Oscillator and phase

The fundamental starts as a sine. Pitch envelopes are applied *in the phase
domain*: we integrate the instantaneous frequency so a gliding pitch stays
phase-coherent and produces no clicks:

```
phase[n] = 2*pi * sum(f0 * pitch_env[n]) / sample_rate
```

### Warmth layers

All warmth components share the same phase, so the octave (`sin(2*phase)`)
stays perfectly locked to the fundamental even while the pitch glides. The
triangle is derived from the phase too:

```
triangle(phase) = (2/pi) * asin(sin(phase))
```

### Saturation and aliasing

When a nonlinearity distorts a sine it creates harmonics. A digital system
*aliases* those harmonics back into the audible band unless we oversample.
The implementation:

1. Oversample 4x (`resample_poly`), which low-passes and interpolates.
2. Apply `tanh` at the high rate.
3. Low-pass and decimate back to 48 kHz.

The anti-aliasing filter is built into the polyphase resampler.

### Tone low-pass

A one-pole low-pass (`y[n] = y[n-1] + alpha*(x[n]-y[n-1])`) keeps the drive
from getting fizzy. The cutoff is linked to drive (more drive -> more
highs to let through) and darkened by weight.

### Soft clipper and limiter

The soft clipper is a tanh curve with a blend amount — essentially linear up
to the threshold, then gently bent. The look-ahead limiter peak-holds the
magnitude over a small window, computes the gain needed to pull peaks to the
threshold, applies it instantly, and releases slowly. Its job is *not*
loudness maximization; it just stops clipping.

Finally the signal is normalized to -0.5 dB headroom so every render peaks
consistently, and written as a 24-bit mono WAV at 48 kHz.

## How to explore

- `bass --preset velvet --output velvet.wav` — render a preset.
- `bass explore` — batch-render dozens of WAVs across the parameter space
  (e.g. `freq38_drive50.wav`).
- `bass analyze velvet.wav` — waveform, spectrum, harmonics, envelope and
  spectrogram figures.
- `bass gallery` — a playable HTML gallery with the CLI command under each
  sound.

The mapping from control to timbre is the interesting part:

- **Frequency** sets the root of everything; pick it musically (a fifth down
  from your chord root usually works).
- **Punch** creates the transient *feel*. Start at 0 and raise it until the
  attack has body but no "boing".
- **Drive** adds audibility on small speakers; raise it until it's audible
  but before it gets harsh.
- **Warmth** adds velvet; it pairs naturally with drive.
- **Weight** is the last knob — set the others first, then use weight to
  make the note as large as the mix can carry.
